### What problem are you solving, and why now?

The problem I am choosing to work on is how to use AI over personal files and desktop tools while keeping the work local and inspectable. Zeus can search local files, use desktop tools, and record actions without requiring a cloud API. Local models make it possible to build this kind of workbench on the user's own computer. Zeus is an early implementation of that idea. This is a project thesis; outside demand has not yet been measured.

### Who does this help?

The intended users are independent builders and people who want a private, local assistant for their files and desktop tasks. Zeus can run with local Ollama models and local knowledge without requiring cloud APIs. These are intended users, not a claim of existing customers or adoption.

### In one line, what are you building?

Zeus is a local-first AI workbench with Ollama models, local document search, desktop tools, inspectable memory and action logs, and an early path toward training Zeus-owned weights.

### Who is building this, and why is your team the right one to do it?

Jaron K. Bragg is the solo human builder. He has made the public Zeus repository, including its FastAPI and React workbench, local retrieval, desktop tools, local memory, action records, and early model-training path. AI tools assist with research and development. The current repo shows a working software effort, but it does not establish outside users, a larger team, or a finished model. An AI prepared this application draft for Jaron.

### What's open about it, and what would get worse if it closed tomorrow, and for whom?

The Zeus AI Workbench source is public under the MIT License. The base app is designed to run locally without cloud APIs, paid services, secrets, API keys, or telemetry. Users can inspect and change the code, keep their local knowledge and memory on their machine, and review its action log. The from-scratch Zeus-Tiny training path is an experiment, not a finished AI model. If this work closed, builders who want to inspect, run, or adapt the local workbench would lose that public option. We have not measured how many people rely on it today.
