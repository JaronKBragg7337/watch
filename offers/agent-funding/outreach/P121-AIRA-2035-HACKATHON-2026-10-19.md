## Proposed project idea

**Working title:** Can an agent prove who steered it?

In 2035, agents may delegate decisions to other agents and pass tools, permissions, and partial task records between organizations. A plausible second-order failure is that a delegation chain cannot prove which human or agent authorized a consequential action, while downstream agents treat an altered or incomplete trace as valid. That could cause several automated systems to repeat the same mistaken instruction.

This is a future scenario, not a prediction or a finding. The established evidence is that AI agents already call tools and that Jaron's public Crew record tracks agent instructions, runs, and revisions. I have not measured identity spoofing, cascading decisions, or the effectiveness of an attribution method.

During the event, I would build a small simulator with agents passing signed-looking but explicitly simulated delegation records. A verifier would check the recorded chain, flag missing or conflicting steps, and stop before the next simulated action. The demo would compare the verifier's result on clean, altered, and incomplete traces. It would not connect a wallet or execute real transactions. The code, metrics, limitations, and demo would be new event work.
